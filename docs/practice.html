Practice

A class with a beginning, a middle, and an end.

You will always know which part of the hour you are in. Nothing is there to catch you out.

We start on the floor or on the feet, depending on the day. The breath comes first. It is a count you can return to when a shape asks for effort.

I name the feeling I want, and the part of the body that does the work. When a shape is unfamiliar, a second version sits beside it. Choosing that version is part of practising well.

We finish still, for long enough that the hour can land. Then one plain suggestion for the week ahead. Often it is a minute of the same breath, on a day you do not come to class.

The hour

What we do, in order.

  1. 01

    Arrive

    A few quiet minutes. You do not need to be calm already. Arriving is the first practice.

  2. 02

    Breathe

    A simple pattern, taught out loud, until it feels familiar.

  3. 03

    Stand and balance

    Strength for the legs and the back, with a wall or a chair when a hand makes the shape steadier.

  4. 04

    Go to the floor

    Hips, back, and belly. Slower here. Room for questions.

  5. 05

    Rest

    Lying down, covered if you wish, with nothing else to do.

  6. 06

    Leave with one thing

    A single practice small enough to repeat at home.

A clear boundary

I do not force a shape. I do not promise that yoga will heal an injury. If something hurts in a sharp way, we stop and we change it. A doctor, a midwife, or a physiotherapist comes before a class.
